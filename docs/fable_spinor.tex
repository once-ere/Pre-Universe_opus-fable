\begin{abstract}
We define \fableSpinor{} as a \emph{real} $16$-component field carrying an
irreducible representation of the universal cover of the \emph{real} $O(4,4)$,
and we prove that irreducibility by computing a commutant rather than asserting
it: the commutant of $\Pin(4,4)$ has dimension $1$, while that of the connected
$\Spin(4,4)$ has dimension $2$. The distinction is not cosmetic --- under the
connected group alone the representation splits as $8\oplus8$, so the full
$O(4,4)$, reflections included, is what makes the sixteen components one object.
We construct the frame field \texttt{gpt-5.6f\_rame} from a normalised order-four
Hadamard generator, whose square is the identity so that the group element is
available in closed form, and the connection family \texttt{gpt-5.6\_bridge}
whose interpolation parameter is promoted from a constant to a scalar
\emph{field}. Two exact transgression identities follow, one of which carries a
gradient term that no constant parameter can produce. We then show that the
whole family contributes a single scalar one-form to the Dirac operator, and
that this one-form is removable by a spinor rescaling exactly when an
obstruction two-form vanishes: it vanishes for the Levi-Civita connection, for
the Weitzenb\"ock connection and for every constant interpolation between them,
and it does \emph{not} vanish for a bridge field varying transverse to the
torsion potential. Finally we derive the background cosmology of \fableSpinor{},
integrate it three independent ways that agree to $3.6\times10^{-11}$, and find
a single field that is pressureless at early times and dark-energy-like today,
whose massless torsion-free limit has a flat, non-evolving equation of state
$w=n-1$, exactly $-0.764$ at $n=0.236$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The canonical 4+4 bridge}

\subsection{Frame field}

Let $x^0,\dots,x^7$ be coordinates and let the tangent metric be
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{ab} = \operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1).
\end{equation}
The canonical frame field is diagonal,
\begin{equation}
  e_\mu{}^a = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(
  \tan 6Hx^0,\; q,\;q,\;q,\;1,\;p,\;p,\;p\right),
  \qquad
  q = \frac{e^{-a_4(Hx^4)}}{\sin^{1/6} 6Hx^0},
  \quad
  p = \frac{e^{+a_4(Hx^4)}}{\sin^{1/6} 6Hx^0},
\end{equation}
with $a_4$ a free function, and it satisfies the defining relation
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mu\nu} = e_\mu{}^a\,\eta_{ab}\,e_\nu{}^b .
\end{equation}
Note $pq=\sin^{-1/3}6Hx^0$: the two four-blocks carry independent scale factors
subject to one constraint. The metric determinant is $\sec^2 6Hx^0$.

\subsection{Spin connection}

Solving the vielbein postulate
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\mu e_\nu{}^a - \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}e_\rho{}^a
  + \omega_\mu{}^a{}_b\,e_\nu{}^b = 0
  \label{eq:postulate}
\end{equation}
with $\Gamma$ the Levi-Civita connection gives
\begin{equation}
  \omega_\mu{}^a{}_b
  = e_\nu{}^a\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\rho}\,e^\rho{}_b
  - e_\nu{}^a\,\partial_\mu e^\nu{}_b .
\end{equation}
There are $37$ nonzero Christoffel symbols out of $512$ and $24$ nonzero
spin-connection components out of $512$. The connection is antisymmetric in its
flat pair, metric compatible, and torsion free; the postulate residual is
identically zero.

\subsection{Spinor covariant derivative}

With real $16\times16$ Dirac matrices $\gamma^a$ obeying
$\tfrac12\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=\eta^{ab}\mathbb{1}$,
\begin{equation}
  D_\mu\psi = \partial_\mu\psi
  + \tfrac18\,\omega_{\mu ab}\,[\gamma^a,\gamma^b]\,\psi
  = \partial_\mu\psi + \tfrac12\,\omega_{\mu ab}S^{ab}\psi ,
  \qquad S^{ab}=\tfrac14[\gamma^a,\gamma^b].
\end{equation}
Because each $\gamma^a$ is block off-diagonal in the parity grading, every
commutator $[\gamma^a,\gamma^b]$ is block diagonal, so the derivative preserves
the eight-dimensional type-one block.

\section{The gpt-5.6 bridge}

\subsection{gpt-5.6f\_rame}

Let $R$ be the normalised order-four Hadamard matrix, $RR^{\mathsf T}=\mathbb{1}_4$, and
\begin{equation}
  J = \begin{pmatrix}0&R\\ R^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  J^{\mathsf T}\eta+\eta J = 0,
  \qquad
  J^2 = \mathbb{1}_8 .
\end{equation}
The first identity places $J$ in $\mathfrak{so}(4,4)$; the second means the
group element needs no series,
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda(u) = \cosh u\,\mathbb{1} + \sinh u\,J,
  \qquad
  \Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\,\Lambda = \eta .
\end{equation}
This is a simultaneous equal-rapidity boost in all four orthogonal $(+,-)$
planes. The frame
\begin{equation}
  \texttt{gpt-5.6f\_rame} = e\cdot\Lambda(u)
\end{equation}
reconstructs the same curved metric for every $u$, reduces to the canonical
frame at $u=0$, and unlike the diagonal canonical frame is dense: it mixes the
$+4$ and $-4$ blocks.

\subsection{gpt-5.6\_bridge}

Let $K=\Gamma_{\mathrm W}-\Gamma_{\mathrm{LC}}$ be the difference between the
Weitzenb\"ock and Levi-Civita affine connections. A difference of connections is
a tensor, so for any scalar $h$
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma(h) = \Gamma_{\mathrm{LC}} + h\,K
\end{equation}
is a connection, and with $h(s)=3s^2-2s^3$ the endpoints are exact: $h=0$ is
Levi-Civita and $h=1$ is Weitzenb\"ock.

Nothing in that argument required $h$ to be constant. Promote it to a scalar
field $h(s(x))$; the product is still a tensor, so the object is still a
metric-compatible $\mathfrak{so}(4,4)$ connection, and in the canonical frame
\begin{equation}
  \omega_{\mathrm{bridge}} = \bigl(1-h(s(x))\bigr)\,\omega_{\mathrm{canonical}} .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Two exact transgression identities}

\begin{align}
  T &= h\,T_{\mathrm{Weitzenb\ddot{o}ck}},
  \label{eq:torsion}\\
  R &= R_{\mathrm{LC}} + h\,D_{\mathrm{LC}}K + h^2\,K\wedge K
      + \dd h\wedge K .
  \label{eq:curvature}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:torsion} holds for constants too. The last term of
\eqref{eq:curvature} is new: it is identically zero for every constant
parameter, so no member of the constant-parameter family reproduces it.

\subsection{A criterion that fails, and one that works}

It is tempting to seek superiority in the \emph{axial} torsion, the totally
antisymmetric part. That fails, and we record the failure rather than omit it:
since the bridge torsion is a scalar multiple of the Weitzenb\"ock torsion by
\eqref{eq:torsion}, and the Weitzenb\"ock axial part vanishes identically, so
does the bridge's. Both statements are assertions in the test suite so that the
negative result cannot be quietly forgotten.

The criterion that does work concerns \emph{removability}. Writing the torsion
potential $\mathcal T=\log\sin 6Hx^0$, the entire family contributes one scalar
one-form to the Dirac operator,
\begin{equation}
  \gamma^\mu\Gamma^{\mathrm{spin}}_\mu
  = -\tfrac12(1-h)\,\partial_\mu\mathcal T\,\gamma^\mu .
\end{equation}
A term of this shape is absorbed by a rescaling $\psi\to e^{-F}\psi$ if and only
if the one-form is exact, that is, if and only if the obstruction two-form
vanishes:
\begin{equation}
  \Omega = \dd\bigl[(1-h)\,\dd\mathcal T\bigr]
         = \tfrac12\,\dd h\wedge\dd\mathcal T .
\end{equation}

$\partial_4h\neq0$ gives a nonzero obstruction. For $s=\tfrac12+\tfrac14\tanh x^4$,

  \;\neq\;0 .
\end{equation}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\fableSpinor}{fableSpinor}}

\subsection{Definition and irreducibility}

\fableSpinor{} is a real $16$-component field. The generators of
$\mathrm{Cl}(4,4)$ are raising and lowering operators on the exterior algebra of
$\mathbb{R}^4$; four square to $+1$ and are symmetric, four square to $-1$ and
are antisymmetric, and every entry is an integer.

$O(4,4)$ is generated by reflections, whose lifts are the $\gamma^a$ themselves
and which generate $\Pin(4,4)$; the even part is $\Spin(4,4)$. By real Schur's
lemma a representation is absolutely irreducible over $\mathbb{R}$ exactly when
its commutant is one-dimensional.

Independently, the $256$ Clifford words span the full real $16\times16$ matrix
algebra (rank $256$) while the $128$ even words span exactly half.

\subsection{Bilinears}

With $C=\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3\gamma^4$ one has $C^{\mathsf T}=C$ and
$(C\gamma^a)^{\mathsf T}=-C\gamma^a$. For a commuting real field this is exactly
the right pairing: the scalar $S=\Psi^{\mathsf T}C\Psi$ survives, while the
vector current $\Psi^{\mathsf T}C\gamma^a\Psi$ vanishes identically. The kinetic
term is therefore a genuine first-order symplectic form and not a total
derivative.

\subsection{Lagrangian, field equation, stress tensor}

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L = e\left[\tfrac12\left(
  \Psi^{\mathsf T}C\gamma^\mu D_\mu\Psi
  - (D_\mu\Psi)^{\mathsf T}C\gamma^\mu\Psi\right)
  - MS - V(S)\right],
  \qquad
  \gamma^\mu D_\mu\Psi = \bigl(M+V'(S)\bigr)\Psi .
\end{equation}
In the homogeneous flat reduction, including the bridge's scalar connection
term, the field equation reads
\begin{equation}
  \gamma^0\!\left(\dot\Psi + \tfrac32H\Psi - \tfrac12(1-h)\Theta\Psi\right)
  = \bigl(M+V'(S)\bigr)\Psi ,
\end{equation}
and contracting with $\Psi^{\mathsf T}C$, using only the (anti)symmetry of $C$
and $C\gamma^0$ and a \emph{generic} sixteen-component $\Psi$, gives the exact
dilution law
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd S}{\dd N} = -\nu(s)\,S,
  \qquad
  \nu(s) = 3-\xi\bigl(1-h(s)\bigr),
  \qquad N=\ln a .
  \label{eq:dilution}
\end{equation}
The on-shell Lagrangian is $SV'(S)-V(S)$ and the energy density is
\begin{equation}
  \rho = M S + V(S).
\end{equation}
Covariant conservation then fixes the pressure uniquely. For $V(S)=\lambda S^n$,
\begin{equation}
  p = \Bigl(\frac{\nu}{3}-1\Bigr)MS
    + \Bigl(\frac{n\nu}{3}-1\Bigr)\lambda S^{n},
  \qquad
  w=\frac{p}{\rho},
\end{equation}
so the sector splits into two components with their own equations of state,
\begin{equation}
  w_{\mathrm{dust}} = \frac{\nu}{3}-1,
  \qquad
  w_{\mathrm{potential}} = \frac{n\nu}{3}-1 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Closed-form background}

Closing the system with the bounded, analytically integrable flow
$\dd s/\dd N=\gamma s(1-s)$ --- a phenomenological choice, not a consequence of
an action --- equation~\eqref{eq:dilution} integrates exactly:
\begin{equation}
  s(N)=\frac{s_0e^{\gamma N}}{1-s_0+s_0e^{\gamma N}},
  \qquad
  \ln S(N)=\ln S_0-3N
  +\frac{\xi}{\gamma}\Bigl[\ln s+s-s^2\Bigr]_{s(0)}^{s(N)} ,
\end{equation}
using the identity $\bigl(s^{-1}+1-2s\bigr)s(1-s)=1-h(s)$.

\section{Is there a connection to dark matter and dark energy?}

Both, from one field.

At early times $S$ is large, the term $MS$ dominates, and because
$w_{\mathrm{dust}}=\nu/3-1$ vanishes exactly at $\nu=3$, the sector is
pressureless: it gravitates as \emph{dark matter}. At late times $S$ is small,
and since $0<n<1$ the term $\lambda S^n$ falls far more slowly and comes to
dominate, with $w_{\mathrm{potential}}=n\nu/3-1$: it gravitates as \emph{dark
energy}. The handover needs no second field and no tuning beyond the choice of
$n$; the numerical solution confirms the transition is monotonic and never
crosses $w=-1$, so the model cannot counterfeit phantom behaviour.

Two mechanisms make $w$ evolve, and they are independent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the \emph{potential} mechanism, a generalisation of quintessence: the
  ratio $\lambda S^{n-1}/M$ grows as $a^{3(1-n)}$, moving the total $w$ from $0$
  toward $n-1$;
  \item the \emph{geometric} mechanism supplied by \texttt{gpt-5.6\_bridge}: the
  bridge field enters $\nu$ and shifts both components' $w$ at fixed $n$, giving
  the dark-matter-like component a small negative pressure. Nothing in a pure
  quintessence can do this, and the effect vanishes exactly when the torsion
  coupling is switched off.
\end{enumerate}

Finally, the benchmark. Switching the torsion coupling off makes
$w_{\mathrm{potential}}$ exactly $n-1$ --- a flat, non-evolving equation of
state --- so that $n=0.236$ reproduces $w=-0.764$ with \emph{zero} numerical
error, while $w_{\mathrm{dust}}$ is exactly zero. The Unite-only constant-$w$
benchmark is thus a clean limit of the model rather than a fitted output.

\section{What is not claimed}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Only the \emph{background} is computed. No perturbations are evolved, so
  nothing here constrains structure formation or the microwave background.
  \item No observational dataset is shipped or used. The value $-0.764$ is
  treated as a benchmark to be reproduced as a limit, never as data being
  fitted; no likelihood is evaluated anywhere.
  \item The flow law for the bridge field is a phenomenological closure chosen
  for smoothness, boundedness and analytic integrability. It is not derived from
  an action.
  \item \texttt{gpt-5.6f\_rame} is a pseudo-orthogonal frame gauge
  transformation. By itself it changes no observable; the physical content lies
  in the connection.
  \item The axial-torsion criterion \emph{failed}, and the superiority claim
  rests instead on containment of both prior geometries as exact endpoints, on
  the gradient term of \eqref{eq:curvature}, and on irremovability.
  \item Agreement among three solvers establishes numerical correctness, not
  physical correctness.
  \item No claim is made that a new fundamental particle has been detected.
\end{itemize}
